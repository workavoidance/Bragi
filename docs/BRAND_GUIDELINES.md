# Bragi Brand Guidelines

> **Status:** Working brand direction, August 2026. Bragi is currently a working product name and may change. The principles in this document should survive a future rename.

## 1. Brand idea

Bragi helps students get their thoughts onto the page.

It is a simple, highly accurate speech-to-text tool designed first for Norwegian schools. It should help students express what they know without typing, spelling, or the mechanics of writing becoming the main barrier.

Dyslexia is an important and highly visible use case, but Bragi is not defined only as a dyslexia product. The broader purpose is to help students turn spoken thoughts into written text accurately and efficiently.

### Working tagline

**Get your thoughts onto the page.**

### Supporting product line

**Highly accurate, local speech-to-text. Free for everyone.**

## 2. Core promise

Bragi should make two promises equally clearly.

### For students

Get what you know into written text without typing getting in the way.

### For schools

Use a private, local, simple, free tool that is easy to understand, approve, deploy, and maintain.

The product should feel like dependable utility, not AI magic.

## 3. What Bragi is

Bragi is:

- highly accurate speech-to-text
- local and self-contained
- private by design
- free to use
- simple to install and operate
- non-generative
- open source
- designed first for Norwegian education
- intended to be low-risk and low-overhead for school IT teams

Bragi transcribes what the student says. It does not generate ideas, rewrite answers, or create work on the student's behalf.

A useful internal sentiment is:

**Test the knowledge, not the typing.**

This is not currently the primary tagline, but it captures an important part of the value proposition.

Another useful brand thought is:

**It doesn't work for your kids. It just lets your kids work.**

This should be treated as creative territory rather than approved public copy.

## 4. What Bragi is not

Bragi should not present itself as:

- a generative AI product
- an AI assistant or chatbot
- a creativity or writing-generation tool
- a complicated accessibility platform
- a commercial SaaS product
- a school administration system
- a hobby project
- a large company when it is not one
- a Norse mythology brand

Do not lead with the word **AI**. If relevant, explain the technology further down the page in technical information. The primary language should be **local speech-to-text**.

Whisper may be named in technical documentation, but it is not part of the headline proposition.

## 5. Audience

### Primary market

Norway.

The website and public product experience should be **Norwegian first, English second**.

The default website language should be Norwegian. English should be available as a secondary language.

Norwegian copy should use clear, neutral Bokmål. Avoid bureaucratic language and avoid overly colloquial wording.

### Primary users

School students using laptops.

### Important use case

Students with dyslexia. Dyslexia should be clearly represented in use-case content, but should not limit the definition of the product.

### Decision makers and influencers

The people using Bragi are not necessarily the people approving it. The brand must also reassure:

- teachers
- school leaders
- school IT teams
- municipalities and education authorities

The product should be easy enough for an individual teacher to discover and use, while also looking credible enough for central approval and deployment.

## 6. Brand personality

Bragi should feel:

- warm
- clear
- capable
- calm
- approachable
- quietly confident
- supportive
- human
- slightly quirky, but never playful at the expense of trust

The personality is closer to a trusted, capable companion than a teacher, chatbot, or corporate assistant.

It should feel **personally motivated but professionally made**.

The project may acknowledge that it began because a parent wanted a better tool for a dyslexic child, but the founder should not be the centre of the brand. Do not draw attention to Bragi being a one-person project, and do not pretend there is a larger organisation behind it.

## 7. Tone of voice

Use plain, human language.

Writing should be:

- simple
- direct
- neutral
- reassuring
- concise
- confident without hype

Avoid:

- corporate marketing language
- procurement jargon
- AI hype
- exaggerated innovation claims
- childish language
- technical detail before it is needed

A useful tone principle is:

**This doesn't need to be complicated.**

Bragi should calmly communicate that privacy, deployment, and assistive technology do not need to be expensive, cloud-based, or wrapped in a large commercial platform.

## 8. Trust story

Schools should quickly understand that there is nothing hidden.

Core trust signals include:

- speech is processed locally
- transcription stays on the device
- no student data needs to be sent to a cloud transcription service
- no account or login is required
- no licence server is required
- no subscription is required
- no licence fee
- no internet connection is required for normal transcription once the required software/model is installed
- open source code is available for inspection
- the product is non-generative
- versioning, privacy, security, installation, and maintenance information are documented

Do not shout about open source as the main selling point. Use it as an understated trust signal, with more detail available for IT teams.

## 9. Accuracy

Bragi may describe itself as **highly accurate speech-to-text**.

Accuracy should eventually be demonstrated with real examples, school feedback, or a simple demo rather than supported only by marketing copy.

A future website demo could show imperfect natural speech being transcribed accurately without generative rewriting or embellishment.

## 10. Language support

The brand should be careful to distinguish what the underlying speech-recognition technology can support from what Bragi itself has verified.

The intended positioning is Norwegian first, English second.

Bokmål and Nynorsk should be named explicitly only when both have been tested sufficiently in Bragi to support the claim confidently. Until then, avoid implying verified Nynorsk accuracy simply because the underlying model exposes Nynorsk language support.

## 11. Visual direction

The design should feel like a polished modern software product landing page.

### Overall style

- clean
- elegant
- generous white space
- designerly rather than institutional
- approachable rather than premium
- minimal decoration
- strong typography
- rich enough to feel like maintained, real software rather than a bare GitHub project

Photography or illustration, if used, should appear lower down the page and prove the use case rather than create generic lifestyle mood.

Example imagery could show a student speaking while text appears on a laptop. Avoid generic smiling-student stock photography unless it has a clear functional purpose.

## 12. Colour

### Base palette

- black
- warm paper-white rather than stark white
- rich red accent

### Working accent

`#B33A3A`

This is provisional and should be tuned visually. The red should feel warm, human, and memorable, not like an error or warning state.

The accent can play a meaningful role in the site rather than being limited to small links and buttons.

## 13. Logo direction

### Mark

The working logo concept is based on the **lowercase single-storey `g`** in Bragi.

The `g` should subtly read as a **head wearing a boom-microphone headset**.

Design characteristics:

- clean
- geometric
- rounded
- human rather than angular
- monochrome-capable
- transparent background
- immediately legible at small sizes
- usable on its own as a Windows/macOS tray or menu-bar icon

The headset idea should be communicated with as little extra detail as possible. A slightly rounded microphone tip can suggest a foam microphone cover and help the `g` resolve into a headset.

The mark must still read clearly at 16-24 px. Clarity is more important than cleverness.

### Wordmark

The remaining letters in **bragi** should use a type style that feels consistent with the icon:

- rounded
- clean
- modern
- human
- approachable

Avoid an overly technical, luxury, or institutional typeface.

## 14. Homepage structure

The first version can be primarily text-led. Visual polish should come from spacing, typography, layout, colour, and the logo rather than invented content or fake social proof.

### Hero

Working English direction:

> **Get your thoughts onto the page.**
>
> Highly accurate, local speech-to-text. Free for everyone.
>
> **Download**  |  **How it works**

The hero should explicitly use the well-understood term **speech-to-text**.

Norway does not need to be mentioned in the hero. Norwegian relevance should be obvious through the default language and content further down the page.

### Early reassurance

A compact set of trust signals may appear near the hero, for example:

- Local processing
- Private
- No account required
- Open source

Free should be more prominent than the no-account message.

### How it works

Keep the basic use case extremely simple:

1. Press the shortcut.
2. Speak naturally.
3. Text appears where you are working.

Do not mix this simple explanation with technical or privacy detail.

### Student value and use cases

Explain that what a student knows should not be limited by how easily or quickly they can type it.

Introduce dyslexia clearly here as a key use case.

Do not broaden the page into a catalogue of other accessibility use cases.

### School reassurance

A later section should make adoption feel straightforward. A working heading is:

**Simple for students. Simple for schools.**

Potential reassurance blocks:

- Runs locally
- No internet required for normal transcription
- No student accounts
- No licence fees
- Non-generative
- Open source
- Simple installation
- Low IT overhead

### Technical details

Technical content belongs further down the page or one click away. This is where Bragi can explain:

- Whisper
- supported operating systems
- Bokmål/Nynorsk verification status
- English support
- installer requirements
- updates and versioning
- privacy and GDPR information
- security information
- source code

The brand should ultimately support both Windows and macOS. Website wording should avoid unnecessarily boxing the product into Windows even if Windows ships first.

## 15. Calls to action

The primary homepage action is:

**Download**

A secondary action is:

**How it works**

Avoid commercial funnel patterns such as:

- book a demo
- contact sales
- start free trial
- pricing plans
- create an account

The product is free, so the most natural invitation is simply to download it and use it.

## 16. Brand objective

The ideal homepage progression is:

1. **That's useful.**
2. **That's remarkably simple.**
3. **They've thought about the things I'd be worried about.**
4. **Why wouldn't we allow this?**

That final feeling is the core institutional brand objective.

## 17. Design principle

When choices conflict, prefer:

**simple over feature-rich**  
**clear over clever**  
**human over corporate**  
**proof over hype**  
**local over cloud-dependent**  
**student expression over generated content**  
**trust over novelty**
